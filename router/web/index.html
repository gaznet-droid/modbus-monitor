Four sets of panels. The 200 watt Garden panel feeds the Garden inverter; its only reading is that inverter's eWeLink plug. The 400 watt Shed and Garage panels and the 275 watt set each pass through a meter into DC. DC feeds the 1000 watt inverter and the Epever, which charges the 24 volt battery that runs the 600 watt inverter. The DC bar shows the average voltage of the Shed, Garage and 275 meters, which is the voltage feeding the 1000 watt inverter; a meter with an old reading is left out. Total solar to house is the three inverters added together; what the Epever takes in is battery charging, shown separately and not taken off. A mains battery charger, switched by hand, sits below the Epever and feeds the 24 volt battery around the right-hand side of the Epever. That plug is a separate mains input and is not part of the solar total. The Shed and garage total on Generation is those two meters only; the 275 watt meter has its own card.

FromToEnergy
How this is worked out

It follows Dad’s wiring: the Shed, Garage and 275 watt panels feed the 1000 watt inverter and the Epever; the Epever and the mains charger fill the battery, which feeds the 600 watt inverter; the Garden panel feeds the Garden inverter. The house figure is the three inverter plugs. The 275 watt panels have no meter, so they are estimated as whatever the 1000 watt inverter and the Epever drew beyond what the Shed and Garage meters measured. Battery discharge is what the 600 watt inverter drew beyond what the Epever and the charger were putting in at the time. Inverters are taken as 90 percent efficient, and the rest is shown as losses, together with any measured solar nothing was drawing. Worked out in five minute steps or longer for long periods; steps without readings from the inverters, the Epever and both meters are left out. It goes back 30 days at most.
